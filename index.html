01 — The honest read

What the two-hour constraint actually buys

A weekly maintenance stop in a Keswick subdivision takes about 7 minutes in the yard plus 3–5 minutes of driving when the route is tight. Call it 10–12 minutes per customer, all in. Two hours a week is therefore a hard ceiling of roughly 9 or 10 customers — and that's counting the invoicing, the texting, and the "can you come Thursday instead" messages inside the two hours.

That is not a failure. Nine customers proves five things you cannot learn any other way: that Keswick will pay $115/month for a Standard yard, that your stop time estimate is right, that people don't cancel in week three, that referrals happen, and that you can physically stand doing it. Every one of those is a number the $5,000 model depends on. Get them cheap, get them fast, then decide whether to buy more hours.

The real decision, deferred to week 12

You are not choosing between "small business" and "$5,000 business" today. You are buying data for about $150 and eight Saturday mornings. At week 12 you'll know whether the route is worth handing to a $22–$25/hour scooper — and that hire, not more of your own hours, is what makes $5,000 possible.

Most of this market won't tell you a price

Four of nine make you fill in a form, phone, or message a Facebook page; a fifth publishes only a $78–$350+ range. Publishing a plain, specific price card is by itself a competitive advantage in York Region — and it costs nothing.

No dedicated service is based in Georgina

Every dedicated operator runs out of Newmarket, Aurora or Richmond Hill, and Woof Waste Warriors doesn't even list Keswick. Locally there's Hiking Tails, scooping at $30 a visit as a side line to walking and boarding, and a Keswick Poop Removal Facebook page with no prices. "I live three streets over, and this is all we do" is the pitch, and it can't be copied.

Don't chase the low end

Woof Waste Warriors' twice-weekly plan works out to $13.85 a visit; Poochy Poo's entry tier is about $18. Both are below the cost of your time once you count driving. Let them have those customers; price at $115 a month (about $27 a visit) and win on being local, flat-priced and reliable.

The credited cleanup is your cheapest weapon

Poop Patrol bills the first clean by the minute; others charge $100–$145 for a one-time cleanup. You charge a flat $89 up front and give all of it back to anyone who starts weekly within 7 days. The customer who converts got it free; the one who doesn't paid about $120 an hour for a 45-minute job. Either way the money arrives before the hardest visit, and a credited cleanup costs about $17 in wages to win a customer.

A 4.5× spread on one plan proves the tiering

Poochy Poo's weekly plan runs $78 to $350+ for the same service — the whole difference is yard size and dog count. That's the market telling you flat pricing is wrong. Tier by yard type from day one (Compact, Standard, Large, Estate quoted) and you'll never have to renegotiate a big-yard customer.

Every other week is priced to sell weekly

A Standard yard is $89 every other week or $115 weekly: $26 more for twice the visits, so weekly sells itself. Every other week lives in the Terms of Service but never in the ads, and the Monthly Reset is quote only.

Steal the photo and the gate

Poochy Poo advertises a photo of the finished yard and confirmation the gate was latched, on every visit. It costs ten seconds and it answers the two things every customer silently worries about. Put it on your flyer.

Rules that protect the route

Quote in person: over 5,000 sq ft, rural lots, 3+ dogs on the cleanup, or a yard untouched for 3+ months. Charge more for heavy backlog, and tell the client the extra before you start.

Never advertise every other week or a Monthly Reset price. The website and ads say only "monthly options available, just ask."

The credit is weekly only. Only the flat $89 is credited, never a backlog charge, and the window is 7 days.

Restarts pay the One-time rate. A cancel-and-restart, or more than 45 days between visits (other than a winter pause or a delay on your side), is a One-time Cleanup with no credit. Otherwise people cancel and restart to collect the credit again.

Monthly Reset: first visit is always the $89 cleanup with no credit, then the Reset price from the next month. Only on streets you already service, on that zone's route day. Billed on the 20th for the next month. No client pauses; to stop, they cancel. Cap it at 10–15% of a route day. Upgrade to weekly within 60 days and the $89 gets credited (script #10).

The winter policy, since you're starting in September

Keep serving into winter for as long as yards stay reasonably accessible. When snow cover or frozen ground stops you from safely finding and removing waste, pause, give advance written notice by email, and resume once conditions allow. Paused weeks aren't billed, and any prepaid portion is credited to the next bill. Describe the condition rather than publishing thresholds like "below 0°C": those trigger on most winter days, and published numbers can be held against you.

After a pause longer than 4 weeks, the first visit back is the $89 Spring Reset, credited to the first month back for weekly clients only. Every-other-week and Monthly Reset clients pay it. Weekly stays the only plan that makes a cleanup free, and monthly clients can't pause all winter and come back to a full backlog for nothing.

The number to watch is minutes per stop

The hiring rule: never let revenue per paid labour hour fall below $100. If it does, the route has spread out; fix it with density or a price increase, not a cheaper hire. $22–$25/hour is what it takes to win someone with a car who could be driving for Uber instead. Raise prices 3–5% for new customers every spring, and let existing customers keep their rate for 12 months.

At 12 minutes a stop the model works. At 18 minutes — big yards, long driveways, a route spread across Keswick and Pefferlaw — the same 52 customers take 17 hours and the whole thing collapses. Route density is the business. This is why every flyer you drop should go on a street where you already have a customer.

08 — Gates

Decide with numbers, not feelings

Each gate has a pass mark and a specific thing to change if you miss it. Missing a gate is information, not failure — the point is to change one variable and re-run, rather than grinding on a broken assumption.

End of week 2

Interest

≥ 10 genuine inquiries from ~60 targeted doors + 3 group posts.

If not — the offer is wrong, not the market. Lead with "initial cleanup free when you go weekly" in the headline and re-post.

End of week 4

Price

≥ 5 paying weekly customers at full price ($115 Standard).

If they only bite at monthly — Keswick's willingness to pay is below model. Re-run the $5,000 math before hiring anyone.

End of week 8

Unit economics

≥ 10 customers, ≤ 1 cancellation, average stop under 10 minutes, ≥ 3 five-star reviews.

If stops run 18 min — your yard tiers are mispriced. Fix the price card, not the effort.

End of week 12

Scale

15+ customers, referrals arriving unprompted, you're at the 2-hour ceiling.

If under 8 customers after 300 targeted doors — stop. Either extend to Sutton and Newmarket, or shelve it until the March spring rush.

09 — Housekeeping

The small number of boring things that are actually load-bearing

Everything here is either legally required or protects you from a bad day. Nothing else on the "start a business" checklists matters yet.

Insurance — before customer #1

General liability, roughly $600–$900 a year. You are entering strangers' back yards, near their gates and their dogs. One gate left open is the entire downside of this business.

HST — register voluntarily on day one

You're a small supplier until $30,000 a year, which you'd cross at about 22 year-round weekly customers, or about 30 with a winter pause. Registering later means raising every customer's price 13% mid-relationship. Register now, quote "+ HST" from the first flyer, and claim the tax back on gas, bags and gear.

Georgina home-based business

Home occupations are permitted in R1/R2 zones at up to 25% of the dwelling's floor area, but the bylaw says the business operates within the home — not the garage or a shed. Storing scoops in the garage is low risk; a five-minute call to Zoning (905-476-4301 ext. 2282) settles it.

Where the waste goes

Default: double-bagged into the customer's own bin — it's their household waste and it's free. Haul-Away is a $20/month add-on on any plan, on request. Do not put commercial route waste into your own curbside bin.

Boot and tool disinfection

Parvo and giardia travel on a scoop between yards. A spray bottle of dilute bleach and 20 seconds between stops. Then say so on the flyer — none of your competitors mention it and every dog owner who's had a sick puppy will read it twice.

Terms of Service, public

Every other week, the 45-day restart rule, the backlog charge and the winter pause live in the Terms, not the ads. Publish it as a page and link it in the footer next to the Privacy Policy.

Money hygiene

Separate chequing account or, at minimum, a dedicated e-transfer email from week one. Bill monthly in advance by e-transfer — it costs nothing, versus about 2.9% on cards, and it eliminates chasing payment entirely. The $89 initial cleanup is paid when booked; Monthly Resets bill on the 20th for the next month, due in 5 days.
